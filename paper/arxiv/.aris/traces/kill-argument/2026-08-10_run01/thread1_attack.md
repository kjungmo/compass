The paper should be rejected because its central formal contribution, P1, is false under its stated assumptions. In §4.6 (Proposition 1, Eqs. (11)–(13); `main.tex` lines 280–306), Eq. (11) establishes only negative mean drift; stationarity, zero mean, and finite variance do not imply an exponential first-passage bound. Consider i.i.d. centered Student-\(t_3\) advantages: they satisfy every stated assumption, yet from \(e_0=0\) the one-cycle switching probability is \(P[D_1\ge \Delta_{\mathrm{floor}}+E_0/dt]\), which decays polynomially, directly contradicting Eqs. (12)–(13). The proof also misidentifies clipping at zero as an “absorbing barrier” (lines 288–291); it is reflecting, since a positive increment immediately leaves zero. With i.i.d. unbounded innovations, threshold crossing occurs eventually with probability one and can have finite expected time, invalidating the paper’s never-hit argument. Remark 2 (lines 312–314) cannot repair this: mixing still does not provide exponential tails without an exponential-moment condition, and the unspecified “effective” coefficient is not a theorem. This failure destroys the paper’s advertised provable anti-oscillation novelty (abstract and lines 52–78). The offline internal ablations in §5.1–§5.3 cannot substitute for that guarantee, especially when all external planner comparisons and physical safety/navigation outcomes remain unmeasured in §5.6 (lines 543–570).
