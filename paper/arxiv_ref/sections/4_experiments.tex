\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}

This section \textbf{reports as results only what was actually measured}. The behavioral consistency, a decision-stream observer statistic, and the per-cycle latency of the decision layer are \textbf{measured} with an offline harness that drives the actual decision core, and are presented in \cref{subsec:main,subsec:ablation} (ablation and $\rho$-sweep) and \cref{subsec:latency} (per-cycle latency); the harness setup and metrics are given in \cref{subsec:setup}, and the sensitivity-analysis design is given in \cref{app:sensitivity}. Performance metrics that require physical simulation or a real robot, the external baselines, and the user study were not measured and are therefore not presented as results; their protocols are specified in the Planned Evaluation of \cref{app:planned}, fixed in advance of measurement, with per-case values still to be bound before any run. No measured value is fabricated.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}\label{subsec:setup}

\noindent\textbf{Harness and hardware.} The measurements in this section are obtained by driving the \textbf{actual decision core} (\code{DecisionCore}) with the \code{compass\_eval} offline harness across 5 reproducible encounter scenarios $\times$ 50 seeds $\times$ 6 variants ($dt = 0.05\,\text{s} = 20$ Hz, horizon 10 s; behavioral rerun CPU AMD EPYC 9V74; historical latency CPU AMD Ryzen 5 5500GT). For a fixed C++ standard library (see Standard-library dependence below), all figures are computed deterministically from the decision stream, and the raw data (\code{compass\_eval/results/ablation\_raw.csv}) are released; \cref{tab:params} lists the default knobs used. What this harness measures is the decision layer's behavioral consistency, a decision-stream observer statistic, and per-cycle latency; performance metrics that require physical simulation (success rate, collision rate, minimum social distance, lateral jerk) and the external baselines / user study are separated into the Planned Evaluation (\cref{app:planned}).

\noindent\textbf{Scenarios and comparators.} The scenarios are ambiguous tie (\code{near\_tie}), transient spike (\code{transient\_spike}), mid-course reversal (\code{mid\_reversal}), clean advantage (\code{clean\_commit}), and intermittent advantage (\code{intermittent}). The measured configurations and internal comparators are the proposed method (full) and $-$hysteresis ($\Dfloor = 0$), $-$progress hardening ($k_\rho = 0$), $-$accumulator (immediate argmin), \textbf{simple dwell} (a continuous-persistence, or debounce, timer in place of leaky accumulation: the challenger must be strictly better for 12 consecutive cycles (0.6~s), any interruption resets the count, and no margin is applied; it is not a post-switch minimum dwell combined with an improvement threshold of the kind in switching-system theory~\citep{hespanha1999dwell} or concurrent work~\citep{li2026bigcbf}; comparison in \cref{subsec:leak}), and a \textbf{synthetic correspondence-loss stress comparator} (historical CSV key: $-$class correspondence). This last comparator randomly selects a label when $|J_R-J_L|<0.10$ and otherwise uses argmin; it also lacks the accumulator, so it is not an isolated removal of class correspondence from \sname. Immediate argmin and simple dwell are separate comparator policies, whereas only $-$hysteresis and $-$progress hardening are knob-only core ablations. The $-$safety-override variant is equivalent to the proposed method in offline scenarios that contain no safety event (its effect lies in the collision regime), so it is excluded from this measurement set; its evaluation belongs to the physical-simulation evaluation (\cref{app:planned}). No external comparator is evaluated. In particular, the harness includes neither a previous-class consistency weight of the kind used by \citet{degroot2024topology} and SHINE~\citep{martinezbaselga2026shine}, nor an opinion-dynamics passing rule~\citep{cathcart2023opinion}, nor a dwell-plus-relative-improvement selector as in concurrent work~\citep{li2026bigcbf}; the 0.6~s simple dwell is the only fixed-dwell rule tested. The results therefore do not show that evidence accumulation outperforms these mechanisms; that comparison is planned (\cref{app:planned}).

\noindent\textbf{Decision-stream observer statistic.} The offline observer statistic is computed by applying the \cref{subsec:observer} Bayesian observer to the decision stream, but because the offline harness has no realized trajectory, the observer here consumes the \textbf{per-cycle committed passing side (the decision stream) as a proxy observation} in place of the kinematic motion cues specified in \cref{subsec:observer}, using log-odds updates to avoid posterior saturation, with a symmetric misread likelihood parameter $\varepsilon$ --- an assumed observation error probability, not separately sampled corruptions of the stream ($\varepsilon = 0.2$, $p^* = 0.9$ in this measurement). This is an additional simplification of the \cref{subsec:observer} kinematic-cue observer (implementation: \code{src/compass\_eval/include/compass\_eval/harness.hpp:170-239}). \textbf{The offline statistic is not $t_{\text{legible}}$ with a fixed $\Delta_{\text{hold}}$.} We report the \emph{endpoint-suffix time} $t_{\text{sfx}}$: the first sample of the maximal final run of samples whose posterior for the final output side is at least $p^*$. A value is reported only if at least 0.30 s of observed follow-up follows that first sample; otherwise, including a crossing at the final sample, the trial is censored at the 10 s horizon. The 0.30 s follow-up length was chosen after the review of the log-odds-only version (PR~12) found 22 final-sample-only crossings; it was not preregistered, and no sensitivity over this length is reported. Because the suffix is anchored at the end of a finite log, $t_{\text{sfx}}$ is neither a fixed positive-hold implementation nor a conservative bound on $t_{\text{legible}}$. It measures the stability of the decision stream, not motion or human legibility; the kinematic-cue observer and human validation are left to the planned evaluation (\cref{app:planned}). The censoring rate is the fraction of trials with no reported $t_{\text{sfx}}$ (right-censored).

\noindent\textbf{Input and counting conventions.} The archived scenario generator supplies unclipped Gaussian perturbations directly to \code{step\_evals}; that distribution is not bounded almost surely. It is a scripted robustness experiment, not an empirical verification of P1/P2's bounded-input hypothesis or the normalized production-cost path. Bounded deterministic/property tests provide separate software checks. All six policy streams use the same switch-count convention $\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}[s_i\ne s_{i-1}]$: the initialized class to first emitted output is excluded for every policy. Thus an argmin choice of L on its first output in \code{clean\_commit} records zero subsequent transitions, not an absence of an initial decision. This boundary convention is retained for archived reproducibility and is distinct from the opportunity scorer's response-event accounting.

\noindent\textbf{Observer result versions.} The original main snapshot at \code{9fe495a} used probability-space updates, whose posterior rounds to exactly 1.0 and then absorbs later reversals. The log-odds-only observer (PR~12, \code{5343d457}) is the saturation-only numerical correction. The current results additionally apply the post-hoc 0.30 s follow-up rule, a metric-definition change. All three versions are retained as raw CSVs in \code{src/compass\_eval/results/observer\_versions/}, together with a per-row comparison of the same 1,500 decision streams; each version column is regenerated from source and checked against its archive, and switch, sign-change and entropy columns are identical across versions. The numerical correction changes 200 rows (all become uncensored). The follow-up rule changes 47 further rows, all in the immediate-argmin and synthetic correspondence-loss comparators (all become censored); the proposed method and the two knob-only ablations are identical under both log-odds versions. These versions must not be pooled.

\noindent\textbf{Standard-library dependence.} The scenario generator draws its perturbations with \code{std::\allowbreak normal\_distribution}, and the synthetic correspondence-loss comparator draws its random label with \code{std::\allowbreak uniform\_int\_distribution}. The C++ standard fixes the \code{std::\allowbreak mt19937} sequence but leaves these distribution algorithms to the implementation, so the realized inputs depend on the standard library. All offline-harness values in this paper (R1 and R5, including those quoted in the abstract, introduction, figure captions and conclusion) are libstdc++ (GCC) results; the archived latency figures are a separate, machine-dependent measurement. Building the unchanged harness against LLVM libc++ (versions 18 and 23, Linux x86-64) changes 425 of the 1,500 rows. For the proposed method and the two knob-only ablations, the switch count, sign-change rate and entropy are identical in all 750 rows, and no encounter exceeds one switch; only $t_{\text{sfx}}$ moves (full: 1.33 to 1.34 s). The comparator values move: immediate argmin goes from 29.74 to 30.22 switches per encounter, the synthetic correspondence-loss comparator goes from 37.20 to 37.24 switches with censoring 71/250 to 65/250, and one simple-dwell \code{near\_tie} encounter gains a switch (0.00 to 0.02 per encounter). In R5 the switched fractions (50/50 up to 0.20 m/s, 0/50 from 0.35 m/s), and hence the blocking interval, are unchanged, and the mean $t_{\text{sfx}}$ values move by at most 0.03 s. In this comparison the qualitative separation is therefore unchanged, but exact digits, including the proposed method's $t_{\text{sfx}}$, are specific to the library. The libc++ outputs are kept separately in \code{src/compass\_eval/results/stdlib\_sensitivity/} and must not be pooled with the canonical results. An external rerun, documented in \code{docs/reviews/omnilink-reproduction-2026-09-14.md}, first exposed this dependence on the historical harness.

\noindent\textbf{Metrics.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Headline (directly tied to the contribution):} decision-switch count per encounter (excluding safety-justified switches --- here ``safety-justified'' is determined by the \cref{subsec:safety} safety-branch log, i.e., a safety-triggered switch fired by $c^* \notin \mathcal{S}$ at the safety branch of \cref{alg:compass}; only switches marked by this log are excluded from the headline aggregate), lateral-command sign-change rate or decision entropy, legibility metrics (time-to-legible and illegibility rate (right-censoring rate) computed with the observer model of \cref{subsec:observer}, plus the human judgments that the Planned Evaluation's (\cref{app:planned}) user study will provide). Offline, only the decision-stream statistic $t_{\text{sfx}}$ and its censoring rate are available.
\item \textbf{Delineation of what is measurable now.} Of the headline items above, those computable from the decision stream (switch count, sign-change rate, decision entropy, and the $t_{\text{sfx}}$ statistic with its censoring rate) are measured in this section (\cref{subsec:main}). Lateral jerk, which requires trajectory realization, and standard (safety/efficiency) metrics (success rate, minimum social distance, etc.), which depend on physical simulation, have their definitions and reporting protocols placed in the Planned Evaluation (\cref{app:planned}).
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Strongly confirmed findings.} (i) \textbf{Oscillation control (core claim).} The immediate-argmin and synthetic correspondence-loss stress comparators produce about 98 decision changes per encounter in the ambiguous-tie scenario, whereas the proposed method maintains commitment at 0 (\cref{fig:oscillation}; \cref{tab:main,tab:scenario}). The two distributions do not overlap in range (proposed method entirely 0 vs. $-$accumulator 98.16$\pm$7.85, non-overlapping observed ranges), so the observed separation is complete within these runs, not a statement about distributional support. Because the core separation in this offline measurement is categorical, a formal test would not change the conclusion, so we omit one here; Mann-Whitney U tests, effect sizes, and confidence intervals are reported for the physical performance metrics of the Planned Evaluation (\cref{app:planned}). The 0.6~s simple dwell also records 0 switches in \code{near\_tie} (\cref{tab:scenario}), so this separation shows that filtering suppresses tie-induced flips; it does not single out evidence accumulation. The two rules differ in \code{transient\_spike}, \code{mid\_reversal} and \code{intermittent}. (ii) \textbf{Spurious-input suppression.} In the transient-spike scenario, the proposed method has 0 switches (spike ignored), while argmin has 2 (entering and returning). The simple dwell has 2, because the 1~s spike outlasts its 0.6~s persistence window. (iii) \textbf{Rapid commitment.} In the clean-advantage scenario, the proposed method has 1 switch, with $t_{\text{sfx}}$ 2.37 s. (iv) \textbf{Decision-stream observer statistic.} The oscillatory variants are frequently censored because the posterior fails to stabilize (argmin and synthetic $-$class censoring 72/250 and 71/250, respectively, under the post-hoc 0.30 s follow-up rule; \code{near\_tie} $t_{\text{sfx}}$ 7.9 s vs. 0.05 s for the proposed method; \cref{tab:legibility}). This is a property of the scored label streams, not a measured human or motion legibility result.

\subsection{Ablation Studies}\label{subsec:ablation}

\input{figures/rho_sweep}

\noindent\textbf{Knob ablations: findings that diverged from the design-stage hypothesis (correction by measurement).} $-$Hysteresis ($\Dfloor = 0$) and $-$progress hardening ($k_\rho = 0$) showed \textbf{no observed regression} relative to the proposed method in these scenarios. Because the leaky accumulator's threshold $E_0$ and leakage already reject zero-mean noise, the primary oscillation-suppression mechanism in this operating regime is not $\Dfloor$ or $k_\rho$ but the \textbf{accumulator} itself. In other words, the design-stage hypothesis, stated before measurement, that ``$-$hysteresis $\to$ switch-count blowup'' does not hold under the leaky-accumulator design, and this is a \textbf{hypothesis correction} provided by the ablation measurements. In \code{intermittent}, the proposed method switches once at 1.55--2.10 s across the 50 seeds (\code{src/compass\_eval/results/README.md}) and has 0/50 censored trials, with mean $t_{\text{sfx}}$ 4.11 s. The earlier report of 50/50 censoring resulted from floating-point posterior saturation, not a late-horizon switch. Computing the same observer in log-odds space removes this numerical absorbing state without changing the decision policy or observer likelihood; these \code{intermittent} values are identical under the saturation-only version and the current version, which additionally applies the separate post-hoc 0.30 s follow-up rule. In the same scenario, simple dwell never completes the justified switch because intermittent advantage keeps resetting the timer (0 switches), but because it remains in the initial \Class, it is scored immediately by the observer (0/50 censored, $t_{\text{sfx}}$ 0.05 s) --- a case where the observer statistic alone masks the switching failure. Simple dwell's total censoring (50/50) occurs not in \code{intermittent} but in \code{mid\_reversal} (after the mid-course switch, the posterior fails to re-stabilize by the end of the horizon), whereas in the same scenario the proposed method maintains commitment (0 switches) and is scored immediately with 0/50 censoring. However, the proposed method's zero switches in \code{mid\_reversal} is the flip side of this same masking effect: at the default knobs the accumulator does not track the late-horizon reversal within the 10 s log (in the scenario script the mean challenger advantage ramps linearly from $-0.25$ to $+0.25$, i.e., $5\times\Dfloor$, by the end of the horizon; verified at \code{harness.hpp:56}), and because the final label equals the initial label the observer scores the stream immediately. We report this as an observed outcome of this scripted experiment, not as a prediction of the analysis. \Cref{prop:p2live} does not explain it: its response bound $N_{\text{resp}}$ is an \emph{upper} bound on the time to switch that applies only while a persistent advantage stays above some $D_{\text{sus}}>\Dfloor$, and it gives no lower bound on response time, so the fact that the window in which the mean advantage exceeds the reachability level $0.23$ (\cref{rem:resptime}) is shorter than $N_{\text{resp}}$ does not imply that no switch occurs. Its converse (no-switch) part needs an almost-sure upper bound $D_k\le\bar D$ with $(\bar D-\Dfloor)dt\le(1-\lambda)L$ over the window, which the unbounded Gaussian perturbations ($\sigma=0.03$) of this scenario do not supply. A noiseless-ramp calculation therefore describes a different input and is not evidence about the noisy runs. Whether the zero-switch outcome is a missed warranted switch, and how long a response to it may take, can only be scored against an independently labelled opportunity with an onset and a response deadline (\cref{app:planned}), which this harness does not supply. The switch-count and observer columns here therefore neither confirm responsiveness nor establish a response failure; they are consistent with the conservatism cost noted in \cref{subsec:switch}. Separately, and not as an explanation of this scenario, clip-induced lockout is a distinct, noise-independent mechanism: whenever $\Eth(\rho_k)>e_{\max}$ no discretionary switch can fire in that cycle regardless of the advantage (\cref{rem:resptime}); it applies to the $\rho$-rate sweep below, whereas \code{mid\_reversal} keeps $\rho\le0.5$ and $\Eth\le0.375<e_{\max}$ throughout. Finally, the $t_{\text{sfx}}$ and censoring-rate figures in this section, obtained from the decision-stream observer of \cref{subsec:setup}, measure the stability of the decision stream, not an independent axis of motion legibility, as already noted.

\noindent\textbf{$\rho$-rate sensitivity and offline transition blocking.} The effect of progress hardening depends strongly on the $\rho$ drive rate. Measuring in the scripted \code{clean\_commit} decision-core harness while raising its progress input $v_{\text{lat}}$ from 0.05 to 0.50 m/s, the proposed method always commits (\cref{fig:rhosweep}) (50/50, $t_{\text{sfx}} \approx$ 2.4 s) for $v_{\text{lat}} \le 0.20$ m/s, but \textbf{switching is blocked (0/50) for $v_{\text{lat}} \ge 0.35$ m/s}. This is because a rapidly saturating $\rho$ raises $\Eth$ to $E_0(1+k_\rho) = 0.60$, blocking even justified switches --- the clip obstruction in \Cref{prop:p2live} gives a noise-independent blocking mechanism: with the scenario's advantage of $+0.40$ the leak equilibrium is $(0.40-\Dfloor)\,dt/(1-\lambda) = 0.583 < 0.60$, in the noiseless constant-advantage model; independently, the clip bound $e_{\max}=0.50<0.60$ blocks every discretionary switch after saturation regardless of noise, whereas in the same noiseless model at $\rho = 0$ the advantage would switch within the upper bound $N_{\text{resp}} = 24$ cycles ($1.2$ s). The noisy runs are not covered by these noiseless calculations; whether the switch wins the race against the rising threshold before the clip lockout is what the sweep measures empirically; the \textbf{offline transition-blocking interval is $v_{\text{lat}} \in (0.20, 0.35)$ m/s}. This is a sensitivity of the scripted input and the core's former simplification $v_{\text{lat}}=|v_{\text{cmd}}|$; it does not demonstrate robot immobility, physical freezing, or goal failure. It motivates separating measured cross-track progress from forward speed (\code{compass\_eval/results/R5\_rho\_sweep.md}).

\subsection{Real-Time Performance and Enumeration Complexity}\label{subsec:latency}

\input{tables/latency}

\noindent\textbf{Enumeration complexity (analytical bound).} The current core enumerates $2^K$ labels with $K=\min(N_{\rm people},K_{\rm cap})$ and evaluates each candidate before forming its safe set. At the default $K_{\rm cap}=3$, that is at most eight labels. This is a configured enumeration bound, not evidence that influence ranking, coarse group merging, or a particular pruning distribution has been implemented. The cost of each evaluation also depends on rollout samples, people, and environment queries; the distribution of the resulting $|\mathcal{S}_k|$ in physical scenes remains unmeasured. The ideal pruning/lifecycle design in \cref{subsec:enum} is not a reason to treat the present loop as evaluating only already-safe classes.

\noindent\textbf{$K_{\text{cap}}$ operating rule.} The current implementation retains the first $K_{\rm cap}$ people in input order as explicit class dimensions, with default value 3. It does not compute an influence ranking or merge overflow people into coarse classes. Input selection and ordering therefore matter. Person-cost and safety queries can still examine the supplied people beyond those label dimensions, but that is not a guarantee of a realized passing-side relationship for them.

\noindent\textbf{Historical finite-batch timing.} The archived \code{compass\_eval latency} experiment measured \code{DecisionCore::step} on an AMD Ryzen 5 5500GT with Radeon Graphics for 20,000 iterations per condition, using all $2^K$ enumerated labels and an all-safe environment fixture. The observed p99/max pairs (\cref{tab:latency}) are 2.6/153.6 $\mu$s at $K=1$, 7.9/356.1 $\mu$s at $K=2$, and 58.6/651.3 $\mu$s at $K=3$. The largest observed maximum is about 1.30\% of a 50 ms (20 Hz) period. These are finite-batch measurements, not a proven worst-case execution-time (WCET) bound or an upper bound on deployment latency. The loop evaluates the enumerated candidates before constructing $\mathcal{S}_k$; its work also depends on all supplied people, rollout samples, and environment-query costs, not only $|\mathcal{S}_k|$. Limiting explicit label dimensions does not independently bound all these costs. This historical measurement does not benchmark the new candidate adapter, live costmap access or the end-to-end ROS 2 pipeline (callbacks, TF, controller dispatch, DDS/executor jitter). Those timings and the physical-scene safe-set distribution remain separate evaluation targets.
